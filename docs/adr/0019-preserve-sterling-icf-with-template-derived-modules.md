# Preserve Sterling ICF language through template-derived modules

Sterling Prospective and Ambispective ICF generation uses one versioned module contract derived from the immutable Sterling Client Template Authority. Each module has a stable identity, authority provenance, ordered placement, a `core`, `conditional`, or `source-bound` applicability class, permitted study substitutions, expected paragraph/list topology, and validation severity. Rendering and validation consume that contract; tests assert its public behavior rather than maintaining a second content policy.

Core participant protections are assembled clause by clause instead of replacing whole client sections with generic drafted prose. `STUDY RESULTS`, voluntary participation and investigator-initiated withdrawal, confidentiality and authorization, authorization duration and revocation, post-study information use, and the public-registry notice therefore remain present even when optional source facts are absent. Source wording overrides controlled defaults only at the applicable module and never duplicates the default.

PHI categories and authorized recipients are derived conservatively from approved study facts and controlled applicability rules and render as genuine Word lists. Research-record-access language remains source-bound. Intervention terminology is resolved from the approved intervention so lens studies use lens language and do not inherit generic drug or product terminology. Missing or malformed core modules block generation with a module-specific diagnostic; unsupported optional/source-bound modules are omitted or warned without becoming new Required Source Inputs.

The immutable Sterling authority remains unchanged. The renderer may clone its heading, paragraph, and numbering patterns into the contracted output, and natural pagination may expand as required content is restored.
