IDENTIFICATION AND DESIGN
Protocol number: GLIDE-27-06.
Full title: Historical Glaucoma Drop Burden and Prospective Daily Visual Function.
Short title: Glaucoma Drops and Daily Function.
Protocol study type: Ambispective.
Registry classification: Observational. Observational model: Cohort.
Time perspective: Other, combining retrospective record abstraction and
prospective research follow-up in the same consenting participants.
Condition: Primary open-angle glaucoma treated with topical pressure-lowering
medication. Healthy volunteers: No. All sexes eligible.
Number of sites: 2, both in the United States.
Enrollment target: 120 unique adults, aiming for 60 per site. These are people,
not eyes, visits, prescriptions or historical records.
No treatment, drug, device, surgery or drop schedule is assigned by research.
There is no randomization, masking, placebo or investigational product.
The exposure is the number of active topical glaucoma drug ingredients already
prescribed in ordinary care at enrollment. Category: Drug, observed only.
Research staff do not start, stop, switch, withhold or supply glaucoma drops.

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES
The study links existing glaucoma care records to newly collected daily-function
ratings and eye-pressure measurements. People using different numbers of drug
ingredients may differ in disease severity and other characteristics; the study
does not establish whether drops cause a change in daily function. No prior
effect estimate, bibliography or validated questionnaire reference is supplied.
Primary objective: Estimate the difference between baseline medication-burden
groups in change in daily visual-function rating from Day 0 to Day 60.
Secondary objectives: Describe Day 60 eye pressure in the selected study eye,
historical documented glaucoma encounters and prospective ordinary-care changes.
Hypothesis: The group prescribed two or more active ingredients at enrollment
will have a smaller improvement in daily function than the one-ingredient group.
This is an exploratory association hypothesis, not an established effect or a
claim of comparative treatment efficacy.

EXPOSURE GROUPS AND STUDY EYE
All 120 people form one observational study population with two exposure groups:
1. One active topical glaucoma drug ingredient prescribed at Day 0.
2. Two or more active topical glaucoma drug ingredients prescribed at Day 0.
A fixed-combination bottle counts as its number of active ingredients, not one
ingredient merely because it is one bottle. Count each distinct ingredient once
across both eyes; do not double-count the same ingredient used in both eyes.
Classify from the clinician's current prescription list before any Day 0 change.
No group size or balance is guaranteed. Recruitment is consecutive, not allocation
to a medication regimen. Record later ordinary-care changes without changing the
baseline exposure group or automatically removing the participant.
One eye contributes pressure outcomes. At Day 0 select the eligible glaucoma eye
with higher measured pressure; if tied, select the right eye. If only one eye is
eligible, select that eye. Keep this eye for all pressure outcomes and historical
eye-specific abstraction. A missing later measurement does not justify switching
eyes. Participant function ratings remain one response per person.

POPULATION AND ELIGIBILITY
Age: 40 through 80 years inclusive at consent. All sexes eligible.
Recruit consecutive eligible clinic patients expressing interest after routine
clinic notices. Declining does not affect ordinary care.
Inclusion criteria:
- Clinician-documented primary open-angle glaucoma in at least one eye.
- At least one active topical glaucoma drug ingredient prescribed at Day 0.
- At least one accessible glaucoma encounter at the enrolling clinic during
  1 January through 31 December 2026, inclusive.
- Able to give personal written consent, answer English-language questions
  without a proxy, attend two clinic visits and complete one telephone contact.
Exclusion criteria:
- Glaucoma surgery within 90 days before Day 0 or already planned during follow-up.
- An active ocular infection preventing the specified pressure measurement.
- Another interventional research study within 45 days before screening or
  planned participation in one during the 60-day prospective period.
- Inability to consent personally or need for a proxy questionnaire respondent.
The minimum period without another interventional-study participation before
screening is 45 days. Screening and enrollment occur together at Day 0.
Pregnancy is not excluded. There are no research pregnancy tests, contraception
requirements, radiation, specimens or study drugs. Adults unable to consent and
children are not enrolled. No LAR, proxy or witness signature is required.
Sample justification: 120 people is a feasible exploratory sample, allowing
about 10% failure to complete prospective follow-up. No formal power calculation,
assumed effect size, confirmatory threshold or guaranteed exposure-group ratio
is supplied. Neither missing historical pressure nor a later ordinary-care
medication change is an additional exclusion criterion.

RETROSPECTIVE COMPONENT
After written consent and authorization, staff abstract existing records only
within 1 January through 31 December 2026 inclusive. No unconsented records-only
cohort and no waiver of consent or authorization are requested.
Abstract encounter dates, documented glaucoma diagnoses, prescribed topical
drug ingredients, pressure measurements with eye and measurement date, and
documented glaucoma surgery. Records are those accessible at the enrolling site.
Do not obtain outside records or extend the historical period to fill gaps.
Historical encounters were ordinary care, not historical research visits. Their
frequency, testing and timing were irregular; do not manufacture a common visit
schedule, historical function score or retrospective symptom questionnaire.
For the descriptive historical pressure value, use the selected study eye's
latest documented pressure in the historical period. If more than one is recorded
on that date, use the last time-stamped value. If times are absent, use the first
value in the clinical entry and document that selection. Preserve the measurement
method as recorded; do not assume all historical values used the research method.
An absent historical value is missing, not normal, zero or a reason to exclude.
Count documented glaucoma encounters per person within the fixed period;
deduplicate entries with the same person, date and encounter identifier. Report
record completeness. No documented surgery is reported as 'not documented' unless
the record explicitly establishes absence; missing records do not establish none.

PROSPECTIVE VISITS AND MEASUREMENTS
Follow-up lasts 60 days from Day 0 enrollment, not from diagnosis or medication
initiation. There are three scheduled research contacts: two in person and one
by telephone. Historical encounters are not added to this count.
Visit 1: Day 0, enrollment, in person, no window, about 50 minutes total.
Obtain written consent before research; confirm eligibility; record age, sex,
glaucoma history and the current prescription list; obtain the daily-function
rating; measure pressure in both eligible glaucoma eyes to select the study eye;
record that selection; ask about assessment-related symptoms. Explain telephone
follow-up. Historical abstraction is later staff work, not another participant
visit or extra time added to the 50-minute visit total.
Visit 2: Day 30 +/- 5 days after enrollment, telephone, about 10 minutes.
Obtain the daily-function rating, ask about ordinary-care medication changes,
glaucoma encounters and assessment-related symptoms. No eye-pressure test, new
drug instruction, specimen collection or device assessment occurs by telephone.
Visit 3: Day 60 +/- 7 days after enrollment, final in-person visit, about
25 minutes. Obtain the daily-function rating; measure pressure in the same
selected study eye; update prescriptions, ordinary-care encounters and symptoms;
and confirm completion. There is no extra exit form or closeout contact.
Measure research eye pressure with clinic Goldmann applanation tonometry under
the clinic's usual safety procedures, in mmHg. At each research measurement take
two readings in the relevant eye and use their arithmetic mean. Day 0 uses both
eligible eyes for selection; Day 60 measures only the selected study eye.
Routine topical anesthetic and fluorescein used for tonometry are part of the
assessment, not a randomized intervention or take-home study treatment.
Ask at Day 0, Day 30 and Day 60: 'Over the past seven days, how well could you
perform your usual daily visual tasks?' Record a single study-specific rating
from 0, unable to perform usual tasks, through 100, able to perform all usual
tasks without difficulty. Higher is better. No validation claim is supplied.
There are no home diaries, adherence targets, app logs, home devices, daily
symptom scores, visual acuity tests, imaging, dilation, blood draws, saliva,
genetic tests, specimen storage or future specimen use.
For a missed contact, make up to two scheduling attempts inside its existing
window. Do not replace a clinic pressure test with a telephone or home test.
An unscheduled clinical visit is ordinary care, not an automatically added
research contact. Record reported ordinary-care events at the scheduled contacts.

ENDPOINTS AND STATISTICS
Primary outcome: Change in daily visual-function rating from Day 0 to Day 60
+/- 7 days after enrollment. Calculate Day 60 minus Day 0 on the 0-to-100 scale.
Positive change means improvement; negative change means worsening.
Secondary outcome 1: Selected study-eye pressure at Day 60 +/- 7 days, in mmHg.
Lower pressure is a lower measured value; no target-pressure threshold or
definition of clinical success is supplied. This endpoint is a level, not change.
Secondary outcome 2: Number of documented glaucoma encounters per participant
during 1 January through 31 December 2026, using the deduplication rule above.
Secondary outcome 3: Number of ordinary-care topical glaucoma regimen changes
per participant from enrollment through Day 60 +/- 7 days. A change is a
clinician-directed start, stop or dose-frequency change; a refill alone is not
a change. Count a same-day coordinated regimen adjustment as one event, even
if more than one ingredient changes. Use accessible records and scheduled-contact
updates, reconcile duplicates and retain unknown status when information is absent.
Day 30 function and the selected historical pressure support descriptive context;
they are not extra registry outcomes. No exploratory or genetic outcome is defined.
Analysis populations: all consented eligible enrolled people with relevant
observed data. The primary change analysis needs both Day 0 and Day 60 function
ratings; people missing that pair may contribute other observed summaries.
Use baseline medication-burden groups even when ordinary care changes later.
Primary analysis: linear model of Day 60 minus Day 0 function including baseline
exposure group, Day 0 function, age and site. Report adjusted mean change by group
and the two-or-more minus one-ingredient adjusted difference, with a 95% confidence
interval. A negative difference means less improvement in the higher-burden group,
not proof of harm caused by drops. Confounding by indication remains possible.
Summarize secondary outcomes descriptively by baseline group. Report pressure
once per person from the fixed study eye; no paired-eye model or per-eye enrollment.
Describe continuous data with count, mean, standard deviation, median and range;
categorical data with counts, percentages and explicit observed denominators.
Use available observations. Do not impute function, pressure, encounters or
medication changes. Unknown change status is not zero changes. Describe missing
data, historical completeness and disposition by site and exposure group.
No multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory or causal claims are authorized.

RISKS, BENEFITS AND ALTERNATIVES
Risks include brief discomfort, tearing, irritation or blurred vision during
tonometry; rare corneal abrasion or reaction to routine assessment drops;
frustration with questions; travel/time burden; and loss of confidentiality.
Participants may pause or stop testing. The clinic uses its usual infection
control and assessment safety procedures. Persistent symptoms or clinical
concerns go to the study doctor and ordinary care. No direct benefit is promised.
The findings may inform future studies of glaucoma care and daily function.
The alternative is ordinary care without joining. Existing treatment remains
under the treating clinician regardless of participation or withdrawal.

COMPENSATION, EXPENSES AND INJURY
Compensation: $30 after completion of Day 0, $10 after the Day 30 telephone
contact and $30 after the Day 60 visit, maximum $70. Each completed contact is
paid separately in cash by the enrolling clinic before departure or, for the
phone contact, at the next clinic visit or by arranged pickup within 14 days.
If a participant withdraws after the phone contact, offer pickup within 14 days;
no further research visit is required to collect payment already earned.
There is no completion bonus and no requirement to complete all contacts to
retain earned compensation. A started but unfinished contact earns half that
contact's amount. No parking, transit, mileage or other expense reimbursement
is provided. Compensation is not a clinical benefit.
The sponsor pays for research assessments and study administration. Research
charges are not billed to participants or insurers. Ordinary care remains paid
under the participant's usual arrangements.
For possible research injury contact the enrolling site's coordinator to reach
its study doctor; the clinic arranges evaluation. Use emergency services for
emergencies. There is no sponsor fund for free injury care or additional injury
compensation. Ordinary treatment may be billed to the participant or insurer.
Do not promise free treatment or waive legal rights.

PRIVACY AND AUTHORIZATION
Collect name, contact information, signed consent, age, sex, relevant glaucoma
records, prescriptions, study-eye selection, pressure values, function ratings,
reported care updates and compensation receipts. No date of birth, Social
Security number, genetic information or specimens are collected.
Each site keeps contact details and its identity-to-code key separately on an
encrypted access-controlled drive; consent and signed cash receipts are in locked
research cabinets. Research assessments carry the study ID. The sponsor and
statistician receive coded study data only, without names, contact details,
identity keys, consent signatures or payment receipts. Cash is paid locally;
the sponsor receives aggregate payment totals, not participant identities.
Authorized site investigators/coordinators may access identifiable records for
study duties. Authorized monitors, the reviewing IRB and legally authorized
oversight bodies may inspect relevant original records for oversight. Sponsor
analysts do not receive identifiers through those review arrangements.
Authorization permits the specified historical abstraction and new study data
collection and expires on 31 August 2027, or earlier written revocation. Expiry
does not extend the participant's planned 60-day research follow-up.
Revoke authorization by writing to the coordinator at the enrolling site's
mailing address below. Stop new abstraction and collection after revocation;
already collected information may remain in analyses and required oversight
records. Ordinary care continues. Withdrawal does not erase earned compensation.
Retain study records, keys and receipts securely for ten years after the
31 December 2027 administrative closeout, then destroy them unless a binding
requirement requires longer retention. Retention does not permit new collection.
No public individual-data sharing, data sale, unrelated future research,
commercial specimen use, specimen banking or optional genetic participation
is planned. No genetic-discrimination or future-specimen consent choice is needed.

WITHDRAWAL, RESULTS AND DATES
Participants may decline or stop without penalty or loss of ordinary benefits
by telling or writing to their site coordinator. A final clinical discussion is
optional; no final research measurement or extra contact is mandatory.
The investigator may discontinue participation at the participant's request,
if assessments cannot be completed, or if the study closes. Record reasons
when available; later medication changes alone are not discontinuation criteria.
Offer individual pressure results at attended clinic visits, explain clinical
concerns and refer to ordinary care. An aggregate plain-language summary is
available on request after analysis. No research genetic result is promised.
