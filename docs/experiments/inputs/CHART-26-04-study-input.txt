IDENTIFICATION AND DESIGN
Protocol number: CHART-26-04.
Full title: Recorded Intraocular Pressure After Routine Cataract Surgery:
A Retrospective Multisite Medical Record Review.
Short title: Eye Pressure After Cataract Surgery.
Study type: Retrospective. Study classification: Observational cohort.
Time perspective: Retrospective. Number of sites: 2, both in the United States.
Condition: Pseudophakia following cataract surgery.
One records-based cohort. Routine cataract surgery and subsequent clinical
care were selected and performed independently of this research. There are no
assigned treatments, comparison products, randomization, masking, placebo or
investigational products. This study neither evaluates an investigational
product nor assigns changes to care.

BACKGROUND AND UNMET NEED
Routine postoperative records contain pressure measurements and prescribing
decisions, but measurement availability and recording times vary. Combining
these existing records may describe pressure change and documented early
pressure elevations without assuming a uniform clinical follow-up schedule.
Unmet need: The participating clinics lack a combined description of recorded
postoperative pressure change, early pressure elevations and pressure-lowering
medication initiation, with missing records and paired eyes accounted for.
No published effect estimate or bibliographic references are supplied.
Primary objective: Describe recorded change in intraocular pressure from the
preoperative baseline to the historical Day 30 assessment window.
Secondary objectives: Describe early recorded pressure elevations and new
pressure-lowering medication prescriptions after surgery.
Exploratory objective: Describe the association between a pre-existing diabetes
diagnosis and recorded pressure change, accounting for baseline pressure and site.
Hypothesis: Eyes of patients with documented pre-existing diabetes will have a
larger mean recorded pressure increase at Day 30 than eyes of patients with an
explicitly documented absence of diabetes. This is an exploratory association
hypothesis, not an established result or a causal effect of surgery or diabetes.

HISTORICAL WINDOWS AND DATE ANCHORS
Eligible routine-care cataract operations: 1 January 2024 through 15 August 2026,
inclusive. Historical record window: 3 October 2023 through 30 September 2026,
inclusive. This includes the preoperative baseline window for the earliest
eligible operation and all postoperative windows for the latest operation.
Historical Day 0 means the operation date for each eye, not research enrollment.
Baseline: Last documented intraocular pressure for that eye within 90 calendar
days before its operation, excluding measurements on the operation date.
Early postoperative window: Days 1 through 7 after that eye's operation.
Day 30 outcome window: Days 21 through 45 after that eye's operation.
These are historical record-selection windows, not scheduled research visits.
All relevant clinical care predates research record abstraction. No additional
follow-up is requested to obtain a missing measurement.

POPULATION, SAMPLE AND ELIGIBILITY
Planned sample: All eligible records, anticipated to represent 240 unique
people and approximately 360 eligible operated eyes across the two sites.
Anticipated site contributions: 140 people at Site 1 and 100 at Site 2.
The eye count is an estimate, not an additional participant target. Actual
counts and reasons for exclusions will be reported. No prospective recruitment,
participant enrollment appointments or direct participant contact are planned.
Age: 18 through 89 years, inclusive, at the first eligible eye operation.
All sexes eligible. Healthy volunteers: No.
Sampling: Consecutive eligible routine-care records within the fixed surgical
window. Each person appears once in the participant count; both eligible eyes
may contribute to eye-level analyses. Link duplicate records across clinics
using a restricted site-to-site matching process before coding the dataset.
Assign a person's site to the clinic of the first eligible operation. The eye's
operating site remains recorded separately. A repeated record is not a new eye.
Inclusion criteria:
- Age 18 through 89 at the person's first eligible operation.
- At least one routine cataract operation from 1 January 2024 through
  15 August 2026 at a participating clinic.
- A source operative record identifying the eye and operation date.
- Records accessible under site authorization and the ethics/privacy decisions
  required before research access.
Exclusion criteria, applied to individual operated eyes:
- Cataract surgery combined at the same operation with glaucoma filtering
  surgery, a glaucoma drainage implant or a retinal operation.
- A source operative record that does not permit reliable identification of
  the operated eye or operation date after source verification.
If both eyes are ineligible, exclude the person from the study cohort. If only
one eye is ineligible, retain the other eligible eye. A missing baseline or
postoperative pressure measurement does not exclude an otherwise eligible eye
from the cohort; it affects only the relevant outcome's analysis denominator.
Prior glaucoma, use of pressure-lowering medication and diabetes are recorded
characteristics, not exclusion criteria. Do not infer a diagnosis from a single
prescription. Pregnancy status is not collected and creates no eligibility
restriction. There is no screening washout or restriction on participation in
another study, because this is a records-only review with no research screening.
Sample justification: Include all eligible records from the fixed period. The
anticipated 240 people/360 eyes is a feasibility estimate; no treatment effect,
prospective loss-to-follow-up allowance or formal power calculation is assumed.

RECORD ABSTRACTION AND MEASUREMENT RULES
Research staff will access records only after required ethics/privacy decisions
and written site permissions. Staff will verify eligibility, link duplicate
records, assign study IDs and abstract the following existing information:
age at operation, sex, eye laterality, operation date, operating site, documented
diabetes status, documented glaucoma diagnosis, pressure-lowering medication
use at baseline, pressure measurements and their dates/methods, and new
pressure-lowering medication prescriptions within 45 days after surgery.
Use the recorded pressure value in mmHg and retain the recorded method.
No research pressure test, vision test, questionnaire, telephone call, clinic
visit, specimen collection or other new participant procedure is performed.
No specimens are accessed, transferred or stored. No genetic information or
laboratory testing is included. No imaging re-reading is planned.
For the baseline, choose the latest measurement strictly before surgery within
the specified 90-day window. In the Day 30 window, choose the measurement closest
to postoperative Day 30; if equally distant, choose the earlier date. For the
early window, use all recorded pressure measurements to determine whether any
value reaches the specified threshold; one eye is counted only once even if
several measurements meet that threshold. For baseline and Day 30 selection, if two pressure values on the selected
date have different times, use the later recorded time. If time cannot resolve
multiple values, retain the source ambiguity as missing for that outcome
rather than choosing a value without a documented rule.
Retain measurements recorded before or after treatment as documented; these
observational summaries do not represent untreated pressure trajectories.
The research abstraction schedule is staff work: eligibility/query review in
November 2026, abstraction through January 2027, and verification/query closure
in February 2027. Do not turn these work phases into participant visits.

OUTCOMES
Primary outcome: Mean eye-level change in intraocular pressure, mmHg, from the
selected preoperative baseline to the selected historical Day 30 value
(postoperative Days 21–45). Change is Day 30 minus baseline; positive change
means a higher recorded pressure, not clinical improvement. Use eyes with both
selected measurements. Report the number of contributing eyes and unique people.
Secondary outcome 1: Proportion of eyes with at least one recorded intraocular
pressure of 25 mmHg or higher on postoperative Days 1–7. Denominator: eligible
eyes with at least one pressure measurement in that window. Multiple qualifying
measurements in one eye count as one eye meeting the outcome.
Secondary outcome 2: Proportion of eyes with a newly prescribed pressure-lowering
medication during postoperative Days 0–45. Denominator: eligible eyes with
medication documentation sufficient to assess initiation in that window.
A new medication means a drug not listed for that eye at baseline; changing the
dose of an existing drug does not count. A prescription does not establish
adherence. Record eye-specific prescribing; an unresolved laterality is missing
for this outcome rather than assigned to both eyes.
Exploratory outcome: Association of documented diabetes status with eye-level
pressure change over the primary outcome interval. Diabetes is classified as
documented present, explicitly documented absent, or unknown. Unknown status
is reported separately and is not recoded as absence.
No other outcomes or treatment comparisons are specified.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS PLAN
Describe cohort flow, exclusions, people and eyes contributed by each site,
baseline characteristics, measurement availability and measurement methods.
For the primary outcome, summarize paired observed pressure change by mean,
standard deviation, median and range. Report a 95% confidence interval for the
mean change using a participant-cluster bootstrap with 2,000 resamples,
resampling people with all their included eyes together.
For secondary proportions, report numerator, available-case denominator and
percentage. Report missingness explicitly. Do not treat absent pressure
measurements as no pressure elevation or an absent medication entry as proof
that no medication was prescribed.
For the exploratory association, fit a linear mixed-effects model of pressure
change with documented diabetes status, baseline pressure, age at first eligible
operation and operating site as fixed effects and person as a random intercept.
Use eyes with complete required model variables and diabetes explicitly present
or absent. Report the association estimate and 95% confidence interval; report
unknown diabetes status separately. No confirmatory hypothesis test, p-value
threshold, superiority conclusion or causal interpretation is specified.
No missing-value imputation. Report outcome-specific available-case eye and
person counts. Report measurement-method distributions; do not invent a
conversion between methods or assume that all routine measurements used the
same device. Report data inconsistencies and the records omitted from each
analysis. Selection bias, incomplete records, care-related measurement timing
and residual confounding limit interpretation.
Software: R, version recorded when analysis is performed; no specific version
is prescribed in this input. No additional sensitivity analysis is specified.

CONSENT, ETHICS AND PRIVACY
Reviewing body: Meridian Independent Research Review Board, independent of the
sponsor and clinics. A waiver of informed consent and a waiver of individual
privacy authorization will be requested for this records-only review. Neither
waiver is granted yet. Research record access and abstraction cannot start
until the required decisions and site permissions are documented. Do not
represent a requested waiver as an approval or invent approval details.
No participant contact or re-consent is authorized by this study plan. If the
reviewing body does not permit the requested records-only approach, revise the
study plan through its normal review process before proceeding; do not add
participant visits or generate an ICF as an automatic substitute.
The principal research risk is loss of confidentiality, not complications from
historical surgery or new research procedures. Routine clinical events are
historical data, not prospective research adverse events. The study introduces
no participant safety monitoring visits, clinical treatment or new SAE collection
program. Research staff refer suspected unauthorized access or disclosure to
the site's privacy officer and PI on the day discovered; they document the
incident and follow institutional notification and IRB reporting requirements.
No specific external reporting deadline is supplied.
At each site, direct identifiers remain in the clinic's secure environment.
Authorized site investigators and abstractors access identifying records only
for eligibility, linkage, verification and correction. Restricted matching uses
name, date of birth and operation details solely to resolve duplicate records;
linkage material is kept separately with access limited to the two site PIs
and their designated data managers. The central study dataset contains study
IDs, age, sex, operating site, laterality, relative postoperative days, recorded
measurements, medication variables and diagnosis flags. Exact dates, names,
contact details and linkage keys are not sent to the sponsor or statistician.
Sites retain exact clinical dates for verification and derive relative days
before central transfer. The sponsor and statistician receive coded data only.
Authorized monitors, the reviewing IRB and legally authorized oversight bodies
may inspect relevant source records under applicable permissions. No public
individual-data sharing, sale, genetic analysis, specimen banking or unrelated
future research is planned. Publications contain aggregate results only.
Study linkage files and the coded research dataset are retained securely for
five years after administrative closeout on 31 March 2027, then securely
destroyed unless a binding requirement requires longer retention. Original
clinical records remain under each clinic's usual retention policy; research
closeout does not authorize their destruction.
There is no individual research-result return or clinical alert service. This
review generates no validated clinical report or new clinical test. Historical
findings do not trigger new participant contact under this protocol. Aggregate
results may be made available through clinic notices after analysis; obtaining
a summary is not an additional research assessment or follow-up requirement.
