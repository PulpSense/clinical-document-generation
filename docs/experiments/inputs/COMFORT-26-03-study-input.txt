IDENTIFICATION AND STUDY DESIGN
Protocol number: COMFORT-26-03.
Full title: Historical Ocular Care, Current Symptoms and Prospective Comfort
After Routine Bilateral Cataract Surgery: An Ambispective Observational Study
With Research Saliva Genotyping.
Short title: Comfort After Cataract Surgery.
Study type: Ambispective.
Registry classification: Observational. Observational model: Cohort.
Time perspective: Other — ambispective, combining retrospective record
abstraction and prospective follow-up in the same consenting participants.
Condition: Pseudophakia following bilateral cataract surgery.
Number of sites: 3, all in the United States.
One observational cohort; no treatment arms or comparator product.
Intervention/exposure name: Prior routine bilateral cataract surgery and its
documented ocular-care history. Exposure category: Procedure/Surgery,
observed retrospectively, not assigned or performed by this study.
Research activities: medical-record abstraction, noninvasive vision testing,
symptom questionnaires and one saliva collection for research genotyping.
The study does not assign surgery, an IOL type, medication or other treatment.
It has no randomization, masking, placebo or investigational product.

BACKGROUND, OBJECTIVES AND HYPOTHESIS
Current comfort after cataract surgery may vary between people with different
documented ocular-care histories. This study links historical clinic records
with newly collected symptoms and vision assessments. Research genotyping is
included to explore possible associations with symptom variation. No prior
effect estimates or bibliographic references are supplied.
Primary objective: Describe change in patient-reported ocular comfort from
enrollment to Day 90 after enrollment.
Secondary objectives: Describe historical ocular-care patterns, current and
follow-up visual acuity, symptom-related unscheduled visits and associations
between historical care characteristics and prospective comfort.
Exploratory objective: Examine associations between selected research genetic
variants and Day 90 comfort; these analyses do not establish clinical utility.
Hypothesis: Greater historical use of clinician-documented ocular surface
treatment will be associated with lower Day 90 comfort scores. This is an
exploratory association hypothesis, not a claim of a treatment effect.

POPULATION AND SAMPLE
Planned enrollment: 90 participants, targeting 30 per site.
Age: 55 through 85 years, inclusive. All sexes eligible. Healthy volunteers: No.
Sampling: Non-probability, consecutive eligible clinic patients invited during
routine contact. Only patients who give written consent enter this study;
there is no separate records-only cohort or waiver-of-consent population.
Inclusion criteria:
- Age 55 to 85 at written consent.
- Bilateral cataract surgery completed before enrollment; second-eye surgery
  occurred between 3 and 18 months before Day 0.
- Both surgeries occurred between 1 January 2025 and 30 September 2026, with
  records accessible at the enrolling clinic.
- Able to consent, complete English-language questionnaires, provide a saliva
  sample and attend the specified future visits.
- Willing to authorize access to the historical records described below.
Exclusion criteria:
- Participation in an interventional research study in the 60 days before
  screening, or planned interventional research participation during follow-up.
- Planned ocular surgery during the 90-day prospective period.
- An ocular condition that prevents reliable completion of the specified
  visual acuity assessments, as determined by the site investigator.
- Inability to consent personally or need for a proxy questionnaire respondent.
Minimum period without interventional-study participation before screening:
60 days. Screening and enrollment occur at the same Day 0 visit.
Pregnancy is not an exclusion criterion. There are no study pregnancy tests,
contraception requirements, radiation, drugs or reproductive interventions.
Children and adults unable to consent are not enrolled. No representative or
witness signature is required under this study plan.
Sample justification: Ninety participants is a practical exploratory sample,
with up to 15% loss to prospective follow-up anticipated. The study has no
formal power calculation or assumed treatment effect. The target is 90 people,
not 90 eyes or 90 historical records. Both eyes may contribute descriptive
vision data, but participant-reported endpoints use one record per person.

RETROSPECTIVE COMPONENT — NO HISTORICAL RESEARCH VISITS
Historical record period: 1 January 2025 through 30 September 2026, inclusive.
After consent, staff abstract relevant existing records within that fixed
period: surgery dates, implanted lens categories, documented ocular diagnoses,
clinician-recorded ocular surface treatments, best-corrected distance visual
acuity, and documented unscheduled ocular visits.
Historical data are whatever was recorded in routine care. There is no assumed
historical questionnaire, saliva collection, consent visit or standardized
research assessment. An absent historical value is recorded as missing.
The record-abstraction task is performed by staff after Day 0; it is not an
extra participant visit. No pre-consent research procedures were performed.
Source surgical and care records remain at the clinic.

PROSPECTIVE VISITS AND PROCEDURES
Prospective participation lasts 90 days after enrollment, with two in-person
visits and one telephone visit. Day counts are measured from enrollment,
not from cataract surgery.
Visit 1 — Day 0 / Enrollment, in person, approximately 70 minutes:
written informed consent before research activities; eligibility confirmation;
authorization for historical records; demographics and current ocular-care
update; best-corrected distance visual acuity for each eye at 4 m using a logMAR
chart and the participant's current correction; comfort questionnaire; one
approximately 2 mL saliva collection by passive drool into a labeled tube;
and enquiry about symptoms during the research assessments.
Visit 2 — Day 30 +/- 5 days, telephone, approximately 15 minutes:
comfort questionnaire, interval ocular-care update, enquiry about unscheduled
ocular visits since enrollment and assessment-related symptoms. No visual
acuity test or saliva collection takes place by telephone.
Visit 3 — Day 90 +/- 10 days / Final Visit, in person, approximately 40 minutes:
repeat visual acuity for each eye, comfort questionnaire, interval ocular-care
update, unscheduled-visit enquiry and assessment-related symptom enquiry.
Confirm completion after these activities. No exit form, fourth visit or
second saliva sample is required.
If a telephone visit is missed, staff may make up to two further scheduling
attempts within its existing window. No substituted home vision testing.
Questionnaire: A study-specific ocular comfort rating for the preceding 7 days,
0 to 10, where 0 means extremely uncomfortable and 10 means fully comfortable.
This is not presented as a validated instrument. The same wording is used at
Day 0, Day 30 and Day 90.
No blood draws, pupil dilation, imaging, study medication or research surgery.
The saliva specimen is mandatory for this study's eligibility; there is no
optional genetic substudy or separate future-use opt-in.
Assessment-matrix clarification: The historical period is a record-review
column, not Visit 0. Saliva collection occurs only at Day 0; visual acuity
occurs only at the two in-person visits.

ENDPOINTS AND ANALYSIS PLAN
Primary outcome: Change in ocular comfort score, 0–10, from Day 0 to Day 90
(Day 90 +/- 10 days). Positive change means improved reported comfort.
Secondary outcome 1: Ocular comfort score, 0–10, at Day 30 +/- 5 days.
Secondary outcome 2: Best-corrected distance visual acuity in logMAR for each
eye at Day 90 +/- 10 days; lower logMAR means better visual acuity.
Secondary outcome 3: Number of participant-reported unscheduled ocular-care
visits between Day 0 and Day 90, collected at Day 30 and Day 90. A visit reported
at both contacts is counted once.
Secondary outcome 4: Proportion with clinician-documented ocular surface
treatment in available records from 1 January 2025 to 30 September 2026.
Exploratory outcome: Association of coded research genotype groups with Day 90
comfort score. Variant selection is specified in a laboratory analysis plan
before the coded outcome dataset is released; no clinical risk score is made.
No additional outcomes are specified.
Analysis plan: Summarize participant flow, historical-record availability,
completion and baseline characteristics overall and by site. For the primary
outcome, report the observed paired mean change, standard deviation and 95%
confidence interval among participants with Day 0 and Day 90 ratings; also
report median and range. Describe Day 30 comfort and unscheduled visits using
counts and appropriate continuous summaries. Report historical-treatment
proportions with an explicit denominator of people with relevant records.
Summarize visual acuity by eye; any pooled-eye exploratory model uses a
participant-level random intercept to account for paired eyes. Explore the
association of historical treatment documentation with Day 90 comfort using
linear regression adjusted for Day 0 comfort, age and site. Exploratory genetic
associations are reported descriptively; no causal or clinically actionable
interpretation and no confirmatory significance claims. Report missing values
and reasons separately for historical and prospective data. Do not impute
unrecorded historical care or missing questionnaire values.

RESEARCH RISKS, BENEFITS AND ALTERNATIVES
Potential research risks: temporary eye fatigue or headache during vision
testing; dry mouth, mild nausea or embarrassment while providing saliva;
discomfort answering symptom questions; inconvenience and travel; and loss of
privacy involving medical or genetic information. Testing can be paused or
stopped on request. Contact the study doctor for persistent symptoms.
Prior cataract operations were ordinary care and are not new research risks.
Genetic information may reveal information about a participant or biological
relatives if confidentiality is lost. This research does not determine family
relationships, ancestry or clinical disease risk, and does not perform whole
genome sequencing. Genetic information is collected: Yes. The applicable
Sterling genetic-information/GINA section is needed.
No direct benefit is promised. The research may improve understanding of
reported comfort and documented care patterns. The alternative is ordinary
eye care without joining the study. Care and benefits are unaffected by refusal.

GENETIC SPECIMENS, PRIVACY AND RETURN OF RESULTS
Each saliva tube carries only a study ID. The clinic ships coded tubes to the
sponsor's contracted research laboratory. The laboratory performs targeted
genotyping and returns coded research data to the study statistician. The lab
receives no participant names, contacts or clinic ID key.
The original saliva and extracted DNA are destroyed within 90 days after
completion of laboratory testing, and no later than 30 June 2027. Specimens
are not banked for future research, shared for other studies or sold. No
commercial development or participant share of commercial proceeds is planned.
The coded genetic data remain part of the retained study dataset.
Genetic testing is research-only, is not validated for individual clinical
decisions and produces no clinical report. Individual genetic results and
incidental genetic findings are not returned. The study does not search for
clinically actionable incidental findings. Consent must clearly distinguish
this policy from the return of routine vision-test results.
Individual visual acuity results are offered at each in-person visit. Clinical
concerns observed during testing are explained and referred to ordinary care.
An aggregate plain-language study summary is available on request after study
analysis is complete. There is no treatment assignment to disclose or unmask.

CONFIDENTIALITY AND AUTHORIZATION
Collected identifiers: name, contact information, date of birth and signed
consent, used for scheduling, eligibility, records linkage and payment.
Coded study data: age, sex, surgery dates, lens categories, ocular history,
care history, acuity, questionnaire responses, unscheduled visits and genotype.
Each clinic keeps its ID key separately on an encrypted access-controlled
drive; paper consent forms are in locked cabinets. Only authorized site
investigators and coordinators access identifying records for their duties.
The sponsor, laboratory and statistician receive coded information only.
Authorized study monitors, the IRB and legally authorized oversight bodies may
review relevant original records for oversight. No public individual-data
sharing, sale, unrelated future research or identifiable publication is planned.
Identifying records, keys and coded datasets are retained securely for seven
years after administrative closeout on 30 June 2027, then securely destroyed,
unless a binding retention requirement requires longer storage.
Authorization duration: Permission to collect new research health information
ends after the participant's final Day 90 assessment, or earlier upon written
withdrawal. Already collected data may be retained and used for study integrity
and required oversight under the retention policy above. This is a finite
authorization; it does not expire only when all retained records are destroyed.
To withdraw authorization, write to the enrolling site's coordinator at the
site mailing address below. New research collection stops upon withdrawal.
If a specimen has not been analyzed, the site requests its destruction; data
already generated or incorporated into analyses may continue to be used.

COMPENSATION, COSTS, INJURY AND WITHDRAWAL
Compensation: $40 for attended Day 0, $15 for completed Day 30 telephone visit
and $40 for attended Day 90, maximum $95. No completion bonus. Earned payments
remain payable after withdrawal. Payment is by check within 30 days of each
completed contact. No parking, mileage, transit or other travel reimbursement.
The sponsor pays for all research testing, saliva collection, research
laboratory work and questionnaires. Routine clinical care remains payable by
the participant or insurer under ordinary arrangements. No research charges
are billed to insurance; the study does not pay for ordinary future treatment.
For possible research injury or persistent discomfort, contact the enrolling
site's study doctor via the coordinator; the clinic arranges evaluation.
Emergency care uses ordinary emergency services. No fund for free injury
treatment or additional injury compensation is established; ordinary treatment
may be billed to the participant or insurer. Legal rights are not waived.
Participants may leave at any time without penalty or loss of usual benefits.
They can tell or write to the site coordinator. A final discussion of symptoms
and ordinary-care needs is offered but optional; no mandatory final research
visit, saliva collection or examination is required after withdrawal.
The investigator may discontinue participation for participant request,
inability to complete assessments, additional ocular surgery or study closure.
Record the reason when available. Leaving research does not stop routine care.
