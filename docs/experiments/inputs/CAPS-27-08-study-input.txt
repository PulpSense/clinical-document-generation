IDENTIFICATION AND PURPOSE
Protocol number: CAPS-27-08.
Full title: Recorded Vision After Routine Laser Capsulotomy.
Short title: Vision After Laser Capsulotomy.
Study type: Retrospective. Classification: Observational cohort.
Condition: Posterior capsule opacification after cataract surgery.
One United States site; one records-based cohort. The laser procedures and all
clinical care occurred independently of this research. No product, treatment,
procedure or visit is assigned. No randomization, masking, placebo or
investigational product is involved.
Background: Routine records may describe vision after laser capsulotomy, but
measurement availability and methods vary. The clinic lacks a summary that
preserves missingness and distinguishes people from treated eyes. No bibliography
or previous effect estimate is supplied.
Primary objective: Describe recorded study-eye distance visual-acuity change
from the selected historical baseline to the historical Day 42 window.
Secondary objective: Describe recorded early pressure elevations in that eye.
No comparative treatment hypothesis or exploratory endpoint is proposed.

POPULATION, INDEX AND HISTORICAL PERIOD
Include all eligible records, anticipated to represent 150 unique people.
Age 50 through 90 inclusive at the first eligible procedure. All sexes eligible;
healthy volunteers: No. Sample consecutive eligible ordinary-care records.
Index laser capsulotomy dates: 1 January through 31 August 2025 inclusive.
Historical records: 2 December 2024 through 31 December 2025 inclusive.
Historical Day 0 is the selected eye's index laser date, not research enrollment.
Inclusion: clinician-documented posterior capsule opacification, routine laser
capsulotomy at this site in the index period, reliably identifiable person,
procedure date and treated eye, age within range, and records accessible under
required permissions. Exclude an eye if its laser was part of an interventional
research protocol or if eye/date cannot be verified. If no eligible eye remains,
exclude the person. Other eye disease is recorded context, not an automatic
exclusion. Missing baseline, follow-up or pressure values affect only the relevant
analysis, not cohort eligibility.
Use one study eye per person: the eye with the earliest eligible laser procedure;
if both eligible eyes were treated on the same date, select the right eye.
Deduplicate patient records before coding. Keep this eye for every endpoint;
never switch eyes to replace a missing value. Count people once, not procedures
or eyes. There is no screening washout or restriction on other-study participation.
Pregnancy status is not collected and creates no restriction. No children.
The 150-person sample is a feasibility estimate for all eligible records, with
no formal power calculation, assumed effect size or prospective attrition rate.

METHODS AND OUTCOMES
After permissions, staff verify eligibility and index selection and abstract
age at procedure, sex, treated eye, index date, recorded ocular diagnoses,
visual-acuity values with correction status/date/method, and pressure values
with date/method. Original records stay at the clinic.
For acuity, use only distance visual acuity explicitly recorded in logMAR with
habitual distance correction. Do not convert Snellen values, infer correction
status or substitute near, uncorrected or fellow-eye acuity.
Baseline: latest eligible value during Days -30 through -1 before index.
Same-day procedure measurements are not eligible baseline values. If there are
multiple eligible measurements on the latest date, use the last time-stamped
value. If time does not resolve the choice, classify baseline as ambiguous/missing.
Follow-up: eligible value closest to Day 42 within Days 28 through 70 after index.
If dates are equally distant, select the earlier date. On the selected date,
use the last time-stamped eligible value; unresolved multiple values are
ambiguous/missing. Do not widen windows or choose a more favorable result.
Primary endpoint: follow-up minus baseline logMAR in the fixed study eye.
Negative change means better recorded acuity; positive change means worse acuity.
Use people with both selected values; report contributing people, mean change,
standard deviation, 95% confidence interval, median and range. This observational
change is not proof of a laser treatment effect.
Secondary endpoint: proportion with at least one recorded study-eye pressure
of 25 mmHg or higher during historical Days 1 through 7. Denominator: people
with at least one pressure measurement in that eye/window. Use all available
values in the window and count each person at most once, regardless of the
number of qualifying readings. No pressure record means missing, not no elevation.
Retain the recorded pressure method and treatment context; do not describe the
values as an untreated trajectory. No secondary pressure change endpoint.
Use available data without imputation. Report missing, ambiguous and unavailable
measurement types separately, with explicit endpoint denominators. No paired-eye
model, adjusted subgroup model, confirmatory significance claim or invented
exploratory outcome is authorized. Selection, measurement and attendance biases
limit interpretation.
The historical windows identify existing records, not scheduled research visits.
No new acuity or pressure test, questionnaire, phone call, clinic visit, imaging
review, specimen collection, genetic test or participant contact occurs.

STAFF WORK, COMPLETION AND PERMISSIONS
November 2026: obtain required decisions/site permission, verify eligibility,
deduplicate and identify study eyes. December 2026 through January 2027: abstract
records. February 2027: resolve permitted source queries and lock the coded data.
March through April 2027: analyze and prepare aggregate findings. Administrative
closeout and archiving end on 31 May 2027. These are staff tasks, not prospective
clinical collection or recruitment phases; the historical period stays fixed.
A record is complete when extraction and permitted queries are resolved, including
explicit missing/ambiguous codes. Study completion requires dataset lock and
completion of the planned analyses and aggregate report. Archiving follows.
If access permission is withdrawn, suspend research access immediately. Resume
only after documented authorization is restored. If that is impossible, stop
further abstraction, preserve records as required and report available data and
limitations. No patient recontact, new test or replacement eye may fill gaps.

PRIVACY, RISKS, BENEFITS AND COSTS
Only authorized site research staff access identifiers for eligibility and
linkage on the clinic's encrypted access-controlled drive. Keep the study-ID key
separately. Analysts and sponsor receive study ID, age at index, relative-day
intervals and coded clinical data, without names, contact details, birth dates,
exact clinical dates or the identity key. Exact dates remain in restricted source
records. Authorized monitors, IRB and legally authorized oversight bodies may
inspect relevant originals under applicable permissions.
Publish aggregate findings only; suppress cells below five and complementary
cells that would reveal a suppressed count. No individual-level public dataset,
identifying case report, sale or unrelated future research is planned.
Retain research data, keys, permissions and abstraction logs for six years after
31 May 2027 closeout; then destroy research copies unless a binding requirement
requires longer. Original clinical records follow the clinic's separate policy
and are not destroyed by this study.
Risk is loss of confidentiality, mitigated by restricted access, separate keys
and aggregate reporting. There is no new physical research risk, direct benefit,
participant payment, reimbursement or new clinical charge. Sponsor funds staff
work and analysis. No new injury-treatment or insurance promise is established.
Suspected privacy incidents are handled by the PI under site policy and required
ethics/privacy reporting arrangements; no reporting-hour deadline is supplied.
There is no participant consent signature or prospective withdrawal visit under
the requested waiver. Site privacy requests follow applicable permissions, with
no promise to reverse already deidentified aggregate findings.
