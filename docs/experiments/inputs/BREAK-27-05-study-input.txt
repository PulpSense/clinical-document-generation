IDENTIFICATION AND DESIGN
Protocol number: BREAK-27-05.
Full title: Scheduled Screen Breaks and Eye Discomfort in Adults With Frequent
Digital Screen Use: A Randomized Prospective Behavioral Study.
Short title: Screen Breaks and Eye Discomfort.
Study type for protocol selection: Prospective.
Registry study classification: Interventional. Primary purpose: Supportive Care.
Allocation: Randomized. Intervention model: Parallel Assignment. Two groups.
Masking: Single, Outcomes Assessor. Participants and the training coordinator
know assignment. The clinic vision assessor is masked. This is not a
double-masked study; participant-reported symptoms cannot be masked.
Number of sites: 2, both in the United States.
Condition: Eye discomfort associated with frequent digital screen use.
Intervention category: Behavioral. No study-assigned drug, device or surgery.
The research assigns a break routine after consent and eligibility confirmation;
it does not merely observe a routine chosen before enrollment.
Group 1: Scheduled screen breaks, target 36 participants.
Group 2: Usual screen-break habits, target 36 participants.
Total enrollment: 72 unique people, target 36 per site and 36 per group overall.
People, not eyes or diary entries, are the enrollment and primary analysis unit.

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES
The study asks whether a scheduled break routine changes eye discomfort during
frequent screen use compared with usual break habits. A benefit is uncertain.
No prior treatment-effect estimate or bibliography is supplied.
Primary objective: Estimate the between-group difference in change in average
eye-discomfort rating from Day 0 to Day 56 after randomization.
Secondary objectives: Describe near-task satisfaction at Day 56, distance
visual acuity at Day 56, and adherence to the assigned break routine during
the eight-week follow-up. Adherence is assessed only in the scheduled-break group.
Hypothesis: The scheduled-break group will have a greater reduction in eye
discomfort than the usual-habits group. This is an exploratory feasibility
study, not a confirmatory efficacy study. Do not present benefit as established.

POPULATION AND ELIGIBILITY
Adults aged 21 through 65 years inclusive at consent; all sexes eligible.
Healthy volunteers: No. Participants must have the specified eye discomfort.
Inclusion criteria:
- Use a digital screen for at least four hours on at least five days per week,
  based on self-report covering the preceding four weeks.
- Average eye discomfort during screen use of at least 4 on the 0-to-10 study
  rating over the preceding seven days at Day 0.
- Able to provide personal written consent, answer English-language questions
  without a proxy, and complete a paper diary.
- Able and willing to attend three clinic visits and one telephone contact.
Exclusion criteria:
- Eye surgery within 90 days before Day 0, or planned during follow-up.
- An active ocular infection or an ocular condition that the investigator
  determines makes the specified assessments unsuitable.
- A planned major change in screen-use work pattern during follow-up.
- Participation in another interventional study within 30 days before Day 0,
  or planned participation in one during the eight-week follow-up.
Screening and enrollment occur together on Day 0. The minimum nonparticipation
period for another interventional study before screening is 30 days.
Pregnancy is not excluded. No pregnancy tests, contraception requirements,
radiation, specimen collection or study drugs are involved.
No children, adults unable to consent, proxy respondents, legally authorized
representatives or witness signatures are included under this study plan.
Recruitment is through consecutive eligible clinic patients who express
interest after routine clinic notices; participation does not affect care.

RANDOMIZATION, ASSIGNMENT AND MASKING
After written consent, eligibility confirmation and baseline measurements,
randomize participants 1:1 using an independent statistician's computer-generated
sequence, stratified by site with randomly varying blocks of four and six.
A secure allocation service releases assignment to the training coordinator
only after eligibility and baseline measurements have been recorded. Clinic
assessors cannot access the allocation list. Do not assign before consent or
alter group membership according to later adherence.
The training coordinator gives scheduled-break participants a printed sheet
and a ten-minute explanation at Day 0: take a two-minute break away from the
screen after each 30 minutes of screen use during screen-use sessions lasting
at least two hours. No app, timer or screen device is supplied or tested.
Usual-habits participants continue their ordinary break habits; they are not
instructed to avoid breaks. Both groups may continue ordinary eye care.
The vision assessor performs the same measurements in both groups without
access to training records or diaries. Participants are asked not to disclose
assignment to that assessor. If assignment is inadvertently disclosed, record
the masking breach; do not remove the participant automatically.
There is no placebo, emergency unmasking procedure or concealed treatment needed
for clinical safety. Clinical staff can access assignment when necessary.

VISITS AND ACTIVITIES
Follow-up lasts 56 days from Day 0/randomization, not from symptom onset.
Four scheduled contacts: three in person and one by telephone.
Visit 1: Day 0 / Enrollment and Randomization, in person, no window, about
65 minutes total. Written consent before research; eligibility confirmation;
demographics, current eye-care and screen-use history; seven-day eye-discomfort
rating; near-task satisfaction rating; binocular best-corrected distance visual
acuity at 4 meters using a logMAR chart and current correction; symptom enquiry;
randomization after baseline assessments; allocation-specific instruction and
paper-diary training. The ten-minute break explanation is included in the
65-minute total, not an additional visit.
Visit 2: Day 14 +/- 3 days, telephone, about 15 minutes. Seven-day eye-discomfort
rating; interval eye-care update; assessment-related symptom enquiry; questions
about diary completion. No visual acuity test, satisfaction rating, new training
session or specimen collection occurs by telephone.
Visit 3: Day 28 +/- 5 days, in person, about 30 minutes. Eye-discomfort rating;
near-task satisfaction rating; binocular best-corrected distance acuity using
the same chart conditions; interval eye-care and symptom updates; review of
paper-diary completeness without changing assignment or the prescribed routine.
Visit 4: Day 56 +/- 7 days / Final Visit, in person, about 35 minutes. Repeat
the Day 28 assessments, collect the completed diary, and confirm completion.
No extra exit form or separate closeout contact is required.
A missed scheduled contact may prompt up to two scheduling attempts within
that contact's existing window. There are no replacement home vision tests.

HOME DIARY AND MEASUREMENTS
Both groups complete one paper diary entry each evening from the day after
randomization (Day 1) through Day 56 inclusive, about two minutes per day.
Each entry records total screen-use hours and whether screen use occurred.
Scheduled-break participants additionally record the number of eligible
30-minute intervals and the number followed by a completed two-minute break.
Usual-habits participants record the number of breaks they naturally took;
they are not given adherence targets. An evening entry is not a clinic visit.
Day 0 has no home-diary entry. Clinic visits are not multiplied by diary days.
Do not invent daily symptom ratings: the diary does not collect symptom scores.
At Day 0, Day 14, Day 28 and Day 56, ask: "Over the past seven days, how severe
was your average eye discomfort while using a digital screen?" The answer is
0 (no discomfort) through 10 (worst imaginable discomfort). Higher is worse.
This is a study-specific single-item rating; no validation claim is supplied.
Near-task satisfaction is 0 (not satisfied) to 10 (fully satisfied), asked
only at Day 0, Day 28 and Day 56. Higher is better on this different scale.
Distance visual acuity is binocular, with current correction, in logMAR;
lower is better. No per-eye outcome or paired-eye model is specified.
There are no research blood draws, saliva, genetic testing, banking, imaging,
dilation, home devices, study medicines or surgical procedures.

OUTCOMES AND STATISTICS
Primary outcome: Change in seven-day average eye-discomfort rating from Day 0
to Day 56 +/- 7 days, on the 0-to-10 scale. Calculate Day 56 minus Day 0;
negative change means improvement, positive change means worsening.
Secondary outcome 1: Near-task satisfaction rating at Day 56 +/- 7 days,
0 to 10, higher indicating greater satisfaction.
Secondary outcome 2: Binocular best-corrected distance visual acuity at Day 56
+/- 7 days, logMAR, lower indicating better acuity.
Secondary outcome 3: Adherence to scheduled breaks during Days 1 through 56,
only among participants assigned scheduled breaks. Calculate 100 times the
number of completed prescribed breaks divided by the number of eligible
intervals recorded across observed diary days. If no eligible intervals are
recorded, adherence is not estimable; it is not zero. Report diary completeness
and days with no screen use separately. Missing diary days do not count as
zero adherence. Do not calculate control-group adherence to the intervention.
No other or exploratory outcomes are authorized. Day 14/28 symptoms support
descriptive trajectory summaries, without creating additional registry outcomes.
Sample justification: 72 people is a feasible pilot sample, allowing about
15% failure to complete follow-up. No formal power calculation, assumed effect
size or confirmatory significance threshold is specified.
Analysis population: all randomized participants with relevant observed data,
analyzed in their assigned group regardless of adherence. Report disposition,
withdrawal and missing data by group and site. No automatic exclusion for
masking breaches or changes in ordinary care; describe those events.
Primary analysis: linear model of Day 56 minus Day 0 symptom change including
group, Day 0 symptom score and site. Report adjusted mean change by group and
the scheduled-break minus usual-habits adjusted difference with 95% confidence
interval. A negative between-group difference favors scheduled breaks.
Summarize continuous observations with count, mean, standard deviation, median
and range, categorical observations as counts and percentages with explicit
denominators. Summarize secondary outcomes descriptively by assigned group,
except intervention adherence which is intervention-group-only. Use available
observations; do not impute missing symptoms or diaries. Report completeness
and missing reasons. No multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory claims or causal
certainty is justified by this feasibility study.

RISKS, BENEFITS AND ALTERNATIVES
Potential research risks are temporary eye fatigue or headache from vision
testing, frustration or embarrassment with questions, inconvenience from
interrupting screen work and diary completion, travel burden, and loss of
privacy. Participants can pause or stop testing. The break routine may be
inconvenient and may not reduce discomfort. It is not a replacement for ordinary
eye care. Persistent or worsening symptoms should be discussed with the study
doctor; clinical concerns are referred to ordinary care. No direct benefit
is promised. Results may help design future studies of screen-break routines.
The alternative is ordinary care without joining; usual breaks and clinical
treatments remain available regardless of participation.

conditional on finishing the study and is not a guaranteed personal benefit.
The sponsor pays for research assessments, printing and allocation services.

paid by the participant or insurer under usual arrangements.
For possible research injury or persistent discomfort, contact the enrolling
site's coordinator to reach its study doctor. The clinic arranges evaluation.
Use ordinary emergency services for emergencies. No sponsor fund for free
injury treatment or additional injury compensation is established. Ordinary
treatment may be billed to the participant or insurer; legal rights are not
waived. Do not promise free care or automatic injury payments.

PRIVACY, AUTHORIZATION AND SPONSOR ACCESS
Collect name, contact information, signed consent, age, sex, eligibility,
screen-use history, ordinary eye-care updates, assignment, visual acuity,
ratings, diary records and reimbursement documentation. No date of birth,
Social Security number, specimen or genetic information is collected.
Each clinic keeps its identifying key separately on an access-controlled
encrypted drive; signed consent and paper diaries are stored in locked research
cabinets. Diary sheets carry study ID only and are returned at clinic visits.
Participants are asked to keep diaries private while at home.
Authorized clinic investigators/coordinators can access identifying records
for study duties. The sponsor's reimbursement team receives participant name,
mailing address, receipts and reimbursement amount solely to mail checks and
reconcile expenses. Thus the sponsor DOES receive limited identifiable
information; do not describe all sponsor access as coded-only. The sponsor's
analysis team and statistician receive coded assessments, with no contact
details or identity key. Authorized monitors, the IRB and legally authorized
oversight bodies may inspect relevant original records for oversight.
Sponsor reimbursement records are stored separately with restricted access.
Study records, identity keys, diaries and reimbursement records are retained
securely for seven years after administrative closeout on 31 December 2027,
then destroyed unless a binding requirement requires longer retention.
Authorization for new research health information ends at the final Day 56
contact or earlier written withdrawal. Retention does not extend collection.
Withdraw authorization by writing to the coordinator at the enrolling site's
mailing address. New research collection then stops; already collected data
may remain in analyses and required oversight records. Ordinary care continues.
No public individual-data sharing, data sale, unrelated future research use,
genetic research, commercial specimens or specimen banking is planned.

WITHDRAWAL, RESULTS AND CALENDAR
Participation is voluntary and may end without penalty or loss of usual
benefits. Tell or write to the site coordinator to stop participation. A final
discussion about symptoms and ordinary care is optional. No mandatory final
research test, diary submission or extra data collection is required after
withdrawal. The investigator may discontinue participation at the participant's
request, if assessments cannot be completed, or if the study closes. Record
the reason when available; do not demand diary completion to retain reimbursement.
Offer individual vision results at each attended in-person visit. Explain
clinical concerns and refer to ordinary care. An aggregate plain-language
summary is available on request after analysis. There is no individual genetic
result, clinical genetic report or treatment unmasking promised at completion.
Recruitment: 1 February 2027 through 30 June 2027. Final participant assessments,
primary completion and study completion anticipated by 1 September 2027,
allowing the final Day 56 window. Administrative closeout: 31 December 2027.
Closeout includes disposition and reimbursement reconciliation, diary/data
queries, dataset lock, analysis, aggregate summary preparation and archiving.
These staff activities do not extend individual follow-up.
