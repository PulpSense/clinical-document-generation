IDENTIFICATION AND DESIGN
Protocol number: RECORD-27-07.
Full title: Recorded Symptoms and Care Changes After Routine Dry Eye Management.
Short title: Symptoms After Dry Eye Care.
Study type: Retrospective. Classification: Observational cohort.
Time perspective: Retrospective. Number of sites: 1, in the United States.
Condition: Clinician-documented dry eye disease.
The study reviews existing routine-care records. It assigns no treatment,
product, procedure or care schedule. There is no randomization, masking,
placebo, investigational product, prospective enrollment or participant contact.
One records-based cohort; no assigned arms or comparison product.
The index event is the person's first qualifying routine-care dry eye management
encounter in the index period. Different ordinary-care choices are recorded
characteristics, not research assignments or evidence of treatment efficacy.

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES
Existing records may describe symptoms and subsequent care, but clinic attendance,
questionnaire completeness and prescribing documentation vary. The clinic lacks
a combined account that separates recorded symptom change from missing follow-up
and undocumented care changes. No bibliography, published effect size or validated
instrument reference is supplied.
Primary objective: Describe change in the recorded dry eye symptom rating from
the index-care baseline to the historical Day 60 selection window.
Secondary objectives: Describe the proportion with a recorded decrease of at
least 20 points and the frequency of documented clinician-directed care changes.
Exploratory objective: Describe symptom change by whether a contact-lens wearer
was explicitly documented at the index encounter.
Hypothesis: People explicitly documented as contact-lens wearers will have a
smaller recorded symptom reduction than people explicitly documented as nonwearers.
This is an exploratory association hypothesis, not an established effect or a
claim that contact lenses or any treatment caused the observed changes.

HISTORICAL PERIODS AND UNITS
Index encounters: 1 January 2024 through 30 September 2025 inclusive.
Accessible historical record period: 18 December 2023 through 31 December 2025
inclusive. This covers the baseline and follow-up windows for all index dates.
Historical Day 0 is the index ordinary-care encounter, not research enrollment.
Baseline selection window: Days -14 through 0 relative to that encounter.
Historical Day 60 outcome selection window: Days 45 through 90 after index.
Care-change observation window: Days 1 through 90 after index.
These are record-selection windows, not scheduled visits or a requirement for
people to attend at those times. All clinical data predate research abstraction.
Staff will not contact people or arrange new care to fill gaps.

POPULATION, ELIGIBILITY AND SAMPLE
Include all eligible records; anticipate 180 unique adults at the single site.
The person is the enrollment and analysis unit. The symptom rating concerns both
eyes together; no eye-level cohort, separate-eye outcome or paired-eye model is
specified. Multiple encounters for one person do not create new participants.
Age: 18 through 85 years inclusive at the index encounter. All sexes eligible.
Healthy volunteers: No. Sampling: Consecutive eligible records in the fixed period.
Inclusion criteria:
- Age 18 through 85 at index.
- Clinician-documented dry eye disease.
- A routine-care encounter during the index period documenting a dry eye care
  plan, including an explicitly documented decision to continue existing care.
- Accessible records identifying the person and index date reliably.
- Access authorized by the site and required ethics/privacy decisions.
Use only the first qualifying index encounter per person. Deduplicate records
using the clinic's patient identifier before assigning a study ID.
Exclusion criteria:
- The person or index date cannot be reliably established after source verification.
- The putative index encounter was part of an interventional research protocol,
  rather than ordinary clinical care.
Missing symptom scores, uncertain contact-lens status and incomplete subsequent
care documentation do not exclude an otherwise eligible person. They affect
only the relevant analysis denominator. Prior surgery, medication use and
ordinary-care changes are recorded characteristics, not automatic exclusions.
There is no research screening, nonparticipation interval, washout or restriction
on other research participation. Pregnancy status is not collected and creates
no eligibility restriction. No child records are included.
Sample justification: All eligible records in the fixed index period constitute
the sample; 180 people is a feasibility estimate. Report actual counts and
exclusions. There is no formal power calculation, assumed effect size, recruitment
quota or prospective loss-to-follow-up allowance.

ABSTRACTION AND SELECTION RULES
After the required permissions, staff verify eligibility, deduplicate records,
assign study IDs and abstract age at index, sex, index date, documented contact-lens
status, the ordinary-care plan, recorded symptom ratings with dates, and subsequent
clinician-directed care changes. Original records remain at the clinic.
The clinic's existing symptom field asks: 'Over the past seven days, how severe
were your dry eye symptoms overall?' It uses 0, no symptoms, through 100, worst
imaginable symptoms. Higher is worse. No validation claim is supplied. Use only
that recorded scale; do not convert other questionnaires, infer a rating from
narrative notes or treat absent scores as zero.
For baseline, use the latest eligible score in Days -14 through 0. A Day 0 score
must be explicitly recorded before the index care decision; otherwise it is not
an eligible baseline. If multiple scores occur on the selected date, use the
latest time-stamped eligible score. If ordering cannot be resolved, mark the
baseline as ambiguous and missing rather than choosing arbitrarily.
For follow-up, select the eligible score closest to historical Day 60 within
Days 45 through 90; ties between dates use the earlier date. If multiple scores
occur on that date, use the last time-stamped score. If times cannot resolve
multiple values, mark that outcome value ambiguous and missing.
Retain the care received as documented. Recorded change does not estimate an
untreated symptom trajectory or prove that index care was effective.
Contact-lens status has three categories: explicitly documented wearer,
explicitly documented nonwearer, and unknown/not documented. Do not infer wear
from a prescription or collapse unknown into nonwearer.
A care change is a clinician-directed start, stop or dose-frequency change of
dry eye medication or a clinician-directed start/stop of a non-drug dry eye care
measure. A refill alone, appointment booking or administrative note is not a
care change. Count one coordinated adjustment on the same date as one event,
even when several measures change. Deduplicate reports by patient, date and
clinical event; retain an uncertain event as uncertain, not a definite change.
A zero-change count requires explicit documentation that care remained unchanged
covering the interval. Absence of a subsequent record alone is unknown, not zero.
No new examination, pressure or vision test, questionnaire, telephone call,
clinic visit, specimen collection, genetic test or imaging re-reading is performed.
There is no specimen transfer, storage, banking or future specimen use.

OUTCOMES AND ANALYSIS
Primary outcome: Recorded symptom-score change, follow-up minus baseline, on
0–100, using the selected baseline and Days 45–90 score. Negative change means
symptom reduction; positive change means worsening. Include people with both
selected scores. Report the contributing number of people, mean change, standard
deviation, 95% confidence interval, median and range.
Secondary outcome 1: Proportion with at least a 20-point decrease in the same
score. Denominator: people with both selected scores; numerator: change <= -20.
This is a supplied descriptive threshold, not a validated responder definition
or a guarantee of meaningful clinical benefit.
Secondary outcome 2: Number of documented clinician-directed dry eye care changes
per person on Days 1–90 after index. Report counts using the stated event rules.
Report separately those with a supported zero count and those whose care-change
status is unknown. Use people with sufficient care documentation as the analysis
denominator; report that denominator explicitly.
Exploratory outcome: Association of index contact-lens status with paired symptom
change. Compare documented wearers and documented nonwearers using a linear model
adjusted for baseline symptom score and age. Report the wearer-minus-nonwearer
adjusted difference with a 95% confidence interval. A positive difference means
less symptom reduction or more worsening among documented wearers, not proof of
harm from lenses. Unknown status remains in cohort summaries but is excluded
from this comparison; report its count and reason.
Use available observed values. Do not impute symptoms, contact-lens status or
care changes. Distinguish missing, ambiguous and explicitly absent information.
Describe availability and reasons for missingness for each endpoint. Missing
scores do not remove a person from the overall cohort or unrelated summaries.
There are no prospective safety endpoints, confirmatory significance claims,
additional exploratory outcomes or causal conclusions. Selection, documentation,
attendance and confounding limit interpretation.

RISKS, BENEFITS, PAYMENT AND CARE
Research risk is loss of confidentiality from record access and linkage. The
research performs no new physical intervention or assessment. Secure access,
separate keys and aggregate reporting mitigate privacy risk. No direct benefit
to people whose records are reviewed is expected; findings may inform future
research and documentation practices. Ordinary care is unaffected.
There is no recruitment, consent appointment, participant payment, compensation,
travel reimbursement, new clinical charge or research treatment. The sponsor
pays staff abstraction and analysis costs. No new research-injury treatment
program or insurance promise is established for this records-only project.
The investigator handles suspected privacy incidents under site policy and
applicable ethics/privacy reporting requirements; no study-specific reporting
hour count has been supplied. Do not invent physical adverse-event collection,
follow-up examinations or new contact obligations.

ETHICS, PRIVACY AND RECORDS
Requested review body: Northfield Independent Research Ethics Board.
Request waivers of informed consent and privacy authorization for the records-only
review. The application will describe privacy protections and the burden and
selection bias of recontacting former clinic patients. No waiver or approval
is currently granted. If permissions are not granted, the investigator must
obtain an authorized revised study plan before research access; this document
does not authorize a switch to contacting patients.
Authorized site research staff access identifying source records and perform
linkage within the clinic's encrypted access-controlled environment. The identity
key is stored separately from the coded dataset. Signed access agreements and
abstraction logs remain at the clinic. Analysts and sponsor receive coded data
only, with age at index and relative-day intervals rather than names, contact
details, birth dates or exact clinical dates. Exact dates remain in restricted
site records for eligibility and window verification. No Social Security numbers
or genetic information are abstracted.
Authorized monitors, the reviewing IRB and legally authorized oversight bodies
may inspect relevant original records for oversight. Reports contain aggregate
results; suppress public cells with fewer than five people and complementary
cells that would reveal the suppressed count. Do not publish individual-level
data or identifying case descriptions.
Retain the coded study dataset, identity key, access approvals and abstraction
logs securely for five years after administrative closeout on 30 June 2027,
then destroy research copies unless a binding requirement requires longer.
Original clinical records follow the clinic's separate ordinary retention policy;
the study does not order their destruction. No sale, public individual-data
sharing, unrelated future research, commercial specimens or banking is planned.
Because this protocol requests a waiver and has no direct participation, there
is no participant signature process or prospective withdrawal visit. Any site
privacy request is handled through the site and applicable permissions, without
promising that previously deidentified aggregate analyses can be reversed.
