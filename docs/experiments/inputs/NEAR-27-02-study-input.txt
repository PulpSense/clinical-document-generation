IDENTIFICATION AND DESIGN
Protocol number: NEAR-27-02.
Full title: Near Vision and Spectacle Use After Routine Bilateral Monofocal
Intraocular Lens Implantation: A Prospective Observational Cohort Study.
Short title: Near Vision After Monofocal IOLs.
Study type: Prospective. Registry study classification: Observational.
Observational model: Cohort. Time perspective: Prospective.
Condition: Pseudophakia following bilateral cataract surgery.
Two exposure cohorts: enhanced monofocal IOLs in both eyes, or standard monofocal
IOLs in both eyes. Lens selection and surgery occur as routine care before
enrollment, independently of this research. The study assigns no treatment,
implant, medication or surgery. There is no randomization, masking or placebo.
Intervention/exposure name: Previously implanted bilateral monofocal IOL type.
Intervention/exposure category: Device exposure observed during routine care;
no study-assigned intervention. Research procedures are noninvasive vision
assessments and questionnaires. Number of sites: 2, both in the United States.

BACKGROUND, OBJECTIVES AND HYPOTHESIS
The clinical question is whether people with these two routinely selected lens
types differ in near vision and reported use of reading glasses. This study
will collect the same assessments at consistent follow-up points in both
cohorts. No prior comparative effect estimate or bibliography is supplied.
Primary objective: Estimate the difference between cohorts in binocular
uncorrected near visual acuity at Week 12 after enrollment.
Secondary objectives: Describe reading-glasses use, near-task satisfaction,
and binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity at Week 12.
Hypothesis: The enhanced monofocal cohort will have better mean binocular
uncorrected near visual acuity at Week 12 than the standard monofocal cohort.
The study is exploratory and descriptive; it is not a confirmatory efficacy
trial and cannot establish a causal effect of lens choice.

POPULATION AND ELIGIBILITY
Planned enrollment: 80 participants, targeting 40 per cohort and 40 per site.
Age: 50 years or older; no upper age limit. All sexes are eligible. Healthy
volunteers: No. Sampling: Non-probability, consecutive eligible patients at
the two clinics until the enrollment target is reached.
Inclusion criteria:
- At least 50 years old at consent.
- Bilateral routine cataract surgery completed before enrollment, with either
  enhanced monofocal IOLs in both eyes or standard monofocal IOLs in both eyes.
- Second-eye surgery occurred 28 to 42 days before Day 0.
- Able to provide written informed consent and complete the questionnaires in
  English without a proxy.
- Able and willing to attend Day 0, Week 6 and Week 12 visits.
Exclusion criteria:
- A multifocal, trifocal or extended-depth-of-focus IOL in either eye.
- A planned additional ocular surgery during the 12-week participation period.
- An ocular condition that the site investigator determines prevents reliable
  completion of the specified visual acuity assessments.
- Participation in another interventional research study within the 30 days
  before Day 0, or planned participation during follow-up.
Minimum period without other interventional-study participation before
screening: 30 days.
Pregnancy is not an exclusion criterion. No pregnancy testing, contraception
requirement or study-specific reproductive precautions are required because
there is no research treatment, radiation or specimen collection.

VISITS, ASSESSMENTS AND PARTICIPATION
Participation lasts 12 weeks after Day 0, with three in-person research visits.
Visit 1 — Day 0 / Enrollment, no visit window, approximately 60 minutes:
written consent before any research assessment; eligibility and routine
surgical-record review; demographic and lens-type collection; binocular
uncorrected near visual acuity at 40 cm using the same clinic near chart and
logMAR scoring; binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity at 4 m using a
logMAR chart; reading-glasses use questionnaire; near-task satisfaction rating;
and assessment-related symptom enquiry.
Visit 2 — Week 6, Day 42 +/- 7 days, approximately 35 minutes:
both visual acuity assessments, reading-glasses use questionnaire, near-task
satisfaction rating, interval ocular-care update and symptom enquiry.
Visit 3 — Week 12 / Final Visit, Day 84 +/- 7 days, approximately 35 minutes:
the same assessments and updates as Week 6, followed by confirmation that
research participation is complete. No separate exit form or extra closeout
visit is required. Study staff may contact a participant once within 7 days
after a missed visit solely to reschedule within its existing window.
There are no blood draws, genetic tests, stored specimens, pupil dilation,
research imaging, home monitoring, study medications or extra surgeries.
Assessment-matrix note: Both cohorts follow exactly the same visit schedule.
Only lens-type exposure differs.
The reading-glasses questionnaire asks how often glasses were used for near
tasks during the preceding 7 days: never, sometimes, often, or always.
Near-task satisfaction is rated from 0 (not satisfied) to 10 (fully satisfied).
The participant's study ends after the Week 12 assessments, or earlier upon
withdrawal or investigator discontinuation. Routine clinical care continues.

OUTCOMES AND ANALYSIS
Primary outcome: Binocular uncorrected near visual acuity, in logMAR, at Week
12 (Day 84 +/- 7 days). Lower values indicate better near visual acuity.
Secondary outcome 1: Percentage reporting never using reading glasses for near
tasks during the preceding 7 days, at Week 12, from the four-category question.
Secondary outcome 2: Near-task satisfaction score, 0 to 10, at Week 12.
Secondary outcome 3: Binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity, logMAR,
at Week 12 (Day 84 +/- 7 days).
Other/exploratory outcome: Change in binocular uncorrected near visual acuity
from Day 0 to Week 6. No additional outcomes are authorized by this input.
Sample justification: A feasible descriptive sample of 80 participants,
targeting 40 per cohort, is planned. Approximately 10% may not complete Week
12, leaving about 72 completers. No formal hypothesis-testing power calculation
was performed; do not invent an assumed effect size or a power claim.
Analysis plan: Summarize enrollment, disposition and baseline characteristics
by cohort and site. Analyze the primary Week 12 outcome with a linear model
including cohort, Day 0 near visual acuity and site; report the adjusted cohort
mean difference and its 95% confidence interval. Report observed continuous
outcomes using count, mean, standard deviation, median and range. Report
categorical outcomes as counts and percentages with stated denominators.
Present secondary outcomes descriptively by cohort, with 95% confidence
intervals where applicable; no multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory claims.
Use available observations for each analysis without imputing missing outcomes;
report missing counts and reasons by cohort. Include all enrolled participants
with relevant observed data. Exploratory analyses are labeled exploratory.

RISKS, BENEFITS AND ALTERNATIVES
Research-related risks: Temporary eye fatigue, dry-eye discomfort or headache
during vision testing; mild embarrassment or frustration answering questions;
time and travel burden; and a risk of loss of confidentiality. Staff can pause
or stop testing at the participant's request. New or persistent symptoms are
referred to the study doctor. Do not describe prior cataract surgery as a
research procedure or present its surgical risks as new study risks.
No direct benefit is promised. Information may help describe patients' visual
experience after routine care. The alternative is not to participate and to
continue the same ordinary clinical care. Declining does not affect care,
insurance benefits or the relationship with the clinics.

PAYMENTS, COSTS AND RESEARCH-RELATED INJURY
Compensation: $30 for each attended research visit, up to $90 for all three.
Payment is earned per attended visit and is not conditional on completing the
study; withdrawal does not forfeit previously earned payments. Paid by mailed
check within 30 days of the visit. No enrollment bonus or completion bonus.
Travel reimbursement: Actual parking or public transit expenses up to $20 per
attended visit with receipts, separate from compensation; no mileage payment.
Research-only assessments and questionnaires are paid by the sponsor. Ordinary
eye care, medications and treatment that would occur outside research remain
the participant's or insurer's responsibility. No research costs are billed
to insurance. No payment to participants is described as a guaranteed benefit.
If research-related injury or persistent discomfort occurs, contact the site
doctor through the site contact below; the site arranges evaluation. Emergency
care is obtained through ordinary emergency services. This study has no
sponsor fund for free injury treatment or additional injury compensation;
ordinary treatment may be billed to the participant or insurer. Participants
do not waive legal rights. Do not promise free treatment or automatic payment.

PRIVACY, AUTHORIZATION AND DATA HANDLING
Collect name and contact details for scheduling/payment; age, sex, lens type,
surgery dates, relevant ocular history, acuity results and questionnaire data.
Assign a study ID at enrollment. Identifying information and the ID key are
kept separately from coded study assessments. Each site keeps its own key in
an access-controlled encrypted clinic drive. Paper signed consent forms are
kept in a locked research cabinet at the site. Authorized site investigators
and coordinators may access identifying records for study duties.
The sponsor and statistician receive coded assessments, with no names, contact
details or ID key. Authorized sponsor monitors, the reviewing IRB and legally
authorized oversight bodies may inspect relevant original records as required
for study oversight. Reports and publications use aggregate results and do
not identify participants. No sale of data, external future research use,
specimen banking or genetic research is planned. Individual participant data
will not be shared publicly.
Study records and the separate ID keys are retained securely for 7 years after
study closeout on 31 January 2028, then securely destroyed, unless a binding
record-retention requirement requires longer storage. Authorization to collect
new health information lasts through the participant's final research visit
or earlier written withdrawal. This does not require deletion of records
already collected and retained under the stated policy.
Participants may withdraw authorization by writing to their site's study
coordinator at the address below. No new research information is collected
after withdrawal. Previously collected information may be retained and used
for the study's integrity and required oversight. Withdrawal from research
does not stop ordinary care. Do not substitute an unlimited authorization term.

WITHDRAWAL, RESULTS AND CLOSEOUT
Participants may stop at any time without penalty or loss of usual benefits.
They can tell or write to the coordinator. Staff offer an optional discussion
of symptoms and ordinary-care needs; no mandatory final research examination,
extra testing or data collection is imposed after withdrawal. The investigator
may stop participation if the person requests it, cannot complete assessments,
undergoes additional ocular surgery or the study is stopped. Record the reason
if available. Earned payments and eligible incurred travel expenses remain due.
Individual visual acuity results are offered at each attended visit. Findings
requiring clinical follow-up are explained and referred to ordinary eye care.
An aggregate plain-language results summary will be available upon request
from the coordinator after analysis is complete; no individual treatment
assignment or unmasking is involved.
Recruitment: 1 March 2027 through 31 August 2027. Final participant assessments
expected by 30 November 2027. Primary completion and study completion date:
30 November 2027, anticipated. Analysis and administrative closeout end
31 January 2028; these activities do not extend individual participation.
After follow-up, reconcile participant disposition, payments and coded data,
resolve data queries, lock the dataset, analyze results, provide the requested
summary and archive records under the retention policy above.
