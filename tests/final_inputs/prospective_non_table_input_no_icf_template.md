The study is titled Narrative Evaluation of the Meridian Monitor in Adults With Chronic Migraine. Chronic migraine creates a high symptom burden, and continuous device-supported monitoring may help characterize treatment response between routine visits.

The primary objective is to evaluate whether the Meridian Monitor improves tracking of monthly migraine days and participant-reported symptom burden over 12 weeks. The study hypothesis is that participants using the Meridian Monitor will show improved symptom tracking completeness and reduced monthly migraine days by Month 3.

This is a prospective, multi-site, single-arm observational device study. The intervention is the Meridian Monitor, and the intervention type is device. Two investigational sites will participate.

The primary endpoint is change in monthly migraine days from baseline to Month 3. Secondary endpoints include device adherence, usability score, and adverse events. Assessments will occur at screening, baseline, Month 1, Month 2, and Month 3, including migraine diary review, device usage download, adverse event assessment, and usability questionnaire. Participants must have at least 30 days before screening without another study.

The study will enroll 80 participants. This sample size allows descriptive estimation across two sites while accounting for expected attrition. Eligible participants are adults 18 to 75 years old with documented chronic migraine and ability to use the Meridian Monitor. Exclusions include concurrent interventional study participation, inability to complete electronic diaries, or investigator-determined safety concern.

Participants may receive reimbursement for study-related travel. The statistical analysis plan will use descriptive statistics and paired comparisons from baseline to Month 3, with safety events summarized by frequency. The study timeline runs from screening through Month 3 follow-up, with total participation lasting approximately 14 weeks.
